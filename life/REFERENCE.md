# street reference notes (what the /live scene must match)

Written from real photos on Wikimedia Commons (viewed 2026-11-15) plus written sources. The scene is blocky and
stylized, but every item below should be recognizable in it. Photo sources:
- Dar es Salaam: Category:Streets in Dar es Salaam ("A Street in Dar es salaam.jpg"), Category:Street vendors in Dar
  es Salaam (8 photos), Kariakoo media search (9 photos)
- Antananarivo: Category:Streets in Antananarivo ("Anonymous street, Antanarivo.jpg" and the category grid)
- Luanda: Commons media search "Luanda musseque" (aerial of a musseque by the water, a traffic jam, apartment blocks)
- written: LSE blog on Dar informal settlements (vendors, dala dala, bajaj on dirt tracks); Mlalakuwa street-use study
  (streets as shops, workshops and meeting places); Goethe-Institut on Luanda zungueiras (vendors with baskets on
  their heads); travel guides on Antananarivo (trano gasy: two-storey brick with wooden veranda, stairs between hills)

## Dar es Salaam (and the Swahili coast: Mombasa, Zanzibar style streets) - style "swahili"
- houses: single-storey row houses, attached or nearly, plastered walls. colors: ochre / sand, white above a painted
  lower band (teal, sea green, sky blue), some pink and pale yellow. low-pitch corrugated tin roofs, rust brown or
  grey, with a deep overhang over the front
- fronts: steel doors painted blue or green, windows with metal grilles, a raised concrete stoop / bench along the
  front of each house (people sit there). satellite dishes on a few roofs
- street: unpaved dirt, grey-brown with orange earth patches, ruts; about two car widths; no curbs, no sidewalk
- poles: wooden power poles with sagging wires along the street
- trees: neem / mango with round canopies between houses; palms in places
- people: vendors walking with basins of fruit on their heads, bicycles loaded with goods, a man peeling oranges at a
  cart, coconut sellers, small painted kiosks; at Kariakoo the street is packed with pedestrians and goods on tarps
- vehicles: bajaji (three-wheel tuk-tuks), boda (motorbikes, often carrying a passenger), daladala minibuses, old
  sedans parked by the walls
- light: strong sun, a warm dusty haze

## Antananarivo (Madagascar highlands) - style "tana"
- houses: two-storey red / terracotta brick, wooden upper balconies or verandas painted teal and green with railings,
  peaked roofs (tin or tile); some pastel plaster
- ground floors: shopfronts with awnings and small hanging signs, teal double doors
- street: tan brick pavers / cobbles, gutters at the side
- people: standing in groups under awnings, leaning against walls, cyclists; jackets (cool: 14-21 C)
- vehicles: small old cars as taxis, minibuses (taxi-be)
- poles with many wires; the city climbs hills (stairways)

## Luanda (and the West / Central African coast) - style "musseque"
- houses: dense single-storey cinderblock houses, many painted bright pastels (pink, sky blue, yellow, mint, white),
  some bare grey block; corrugated tin roofs, silver and rusted, flat or low pitch
- ground: red earth / sand (musseque = red sand)
- people: zungueiras walking with plastic basins on their heads, small stalls (quitandas) under umbrellas
- vehicles: blue-and-white candongueiro minibuses, heavy car traffic on the main roads

## everywhere
- people stand, walk, sit on chairs, stools or stoops, talk facing each other. babies are carried (on the back in a
  wrap or in arms) or in a cot with a caregiver, never alone. nobody sits on the ground facing a wall
- plastic chairs (red, blue, white), yellow water jerrycans, laundry on lines, potted plants by doors
- signs are generic painted boards (no real brands)
